Electrical Services for Every Home

A tripping breaker can mean an overloaded circuit, a failing breaker, or a panel that has run out of capacity. That is why the work starts with diagnosis, not guesswork.

Electrical panel upgrades are the most common major job. Many Staten Island homes still run on 60-amp or 100-amp service, while modern life needs far more. A 200-amp load centre provides 48,000 watts at 240 volts, enough for central air conditioning, heat pumps, induction cooking and EV charging. Upgrades follow a load calculation under National Electrical Code Article 220, with a service layout request through the Con Edison Project Center and a permit through DOB NOW: Build.

Emergency electrician services handle the problems that cannot wait: a burning smell, a buzzing panel, arcing, or a total loss of power. The dispatch desk triages the risk on the phone first, before sending the nearest mobile unit.

Whole home rewiring is for the borough’s oldest houses, especially pre-1940 homes in St. George, Stapleton and Port Richmond, where knob-and-tube wiring still hides inside lath-and-plaster walls. Rewiring replaces those circuits with modern grounded copper wiring.

Circuit breaker repair covers breakers that trip again and again, hum, or feel hot. Technicians test whether the cause is a branch circuit overload, a ground fault, a short circuit, or a failing panel, using thermal imaging and calibrated meters.

EV charger installation adds a dedicated Level 2 charging circuit for your home. A standard 48-amp charger normally needs a dedicated 60-amp, 240-volt circuit, and on a 100-amp service that often means a panel upgrade to 200 amps first.

Standby generator installation keeps essential circuits running during an outage, with code-compliant manual or automatic transfer switches.

Lighting, outlets and switches, and general repair cover the everyday work too: LED retrofits, recessed lighting, GFCI outlet upgrades, two-prong outlet replacement, and fault finding.

Why Staten Island Needs Local Electrical Expertise

Staten Island is not one kind of housing. North Shore historic homes have knob-and-tube wiring. Mid-century houses in Castleton Corners and Sunnyside have 100-amp split-bus panels with no AFCI or GFCI protection. South Shore homes in Tottenville and Annadale are modern but overloaded by air conditioning, heat pumps and EV chargers. East Shore homes face salt-air corrosion and flood damage after Superstorm Sandy.

Local work also means local rules. All major electrical work in New York City must be performed under permits filed through DOB NOW: Build, and finished installations are reviewed for a Certificate of Electrical Inspection. Service entrance work must follow the Con Edison Blue Book, including meter pan heights and Project Center approval before any ampacity increase.

How a Visit Works

Every visit follows the same four-step process. First, the dispatch desk takes the property’s age and symptoms and triages any immediate safety risk. Second, a technician runs on-site diagnostics: voltage drop testing, thermal imaging of breaker terminals, grounding and neutral checks, and circuit identification. Third, you receive a written, itemised estimate covering parts, labour and permits, and work starts only after your written authorisation. Fourth, the work is completed with commercial-grade materials and tested again, including AFCI and GFCI trip times, followed by a HEPA-filtered vacuum cleanup.

Warning Signs to Call an Electrician

Call the dispatch desk if you notice any of these: breakers that trip repeatedly, lights that flicker when a large appliance starts, outlets that feel warm, a buzzing panel, a burning plastic smell, two-prong outlets, or a panel with no spare spaces. If there is active smoke or flame, leave the property immediately and call 911. Do not open a main panel yourself.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does an electrician cost in Staten Island?

There is no single price, because every job is different. After diagnostics, you receive a written, itemised estimate covering parts, labour and permits, and work begins only after your written authorisation.

Do I need a permit for electrical work in Staten Island?

Yes, for major work. Service panel upgrades, whole-home rewiring, dedicated equipment feeds and commercial fit-outs must be performed under permits filed through DOB NOW: Build, and finished work is reviewed for a Certificate of Electrical Inspection.

How long does a panel upgrade take?

The physical installation of a 200-amp panel, new meter pan and grounding system typically takes 6 to 8 hours of on-site work. The full project takes longer, because it includes load calculations, a Con Edison filing, a permit, and a scheduled utility disconnect and reconnect.

Can I install an EV charger on a 100-amp panel?

Lower-amperage Level 2 chargers can sometimes run on 100-amp service, but a standard 48-amp charger needs a dedicated 60-amp, 240-volt circuit, which usually triggers a panel upgrade to 200 amps.

What should I do if my panel is buzzing or smells like burning plastic?
